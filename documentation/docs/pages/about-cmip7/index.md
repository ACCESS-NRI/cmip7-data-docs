# About CMIP7

The Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP) brings together climate modelling centres from around the world to run the same experiments under shared protocols, so their results can be directly compared. Coordinated by the [World Climate Research Programme (WCRP)](https://www.wcrp-climate.org/), CMIP's data is openly available through the Earth System Grid Federation (ESGF) and underpins IPCC assessments, national climate projections and a wide range of climate research.

CMIP7 is the seventh phase of the project. Its first results will inform the IPCC's Seventh Assessment Report (AR7).

## What is CMIP7?

??? info "Why CMIP7 matters"

    #### Why CMIP7 matters

    CMIP is the shared global evidence base for understanding how the climate is changing and how it may change in future. Almost every major climate projection, assessment and risk study draws on it.

    ##### For policy and decision-makers

    - **It underpins the IPCC.** CMIP7's Assessment Fast Track is designed to feed directly into the IPCC's Seventh Assessment Report (AR7), which governments rely on for international climate negotiations and targets.
    - **It informs national planning.** National climate projections and risk assessments, including Australia's, are built on CMIP data. They shape decisions on infrastructure, water, agriculture, health, and disaster preparedness.
    - **It links emissions to outcomes.** CMIP7's focus on emissions-driven experiments helps connect emissions pathways more directly to climate outcomes, including what happens to warming and extremes as the climate stabilises.
    - **It aims to be more timely.** More regular updates to forcings and data mean climate information can keep pace with the real world, rather than waiting years for each new phase.

    ##### For researchers

    - **It's the world's largest coordinated set of climate simulations.** Running many models under the same conditions lets you compare them, quantify uncertainty and identify robust results.
    - **It's open and standardised.** All data is freely available in a common format, making it easy to combine models.
    - **It's a foundation for further work.** CMIP output feeds into regional downscaling, impacts and adaptation studies, and model development, including for Australia's ACCESS models.

    ##### The big questions

    CMIP7 is guided by four research questions, set out in the [CMIP7 overview paper (Dunne et al., 2025)](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/6671/2025/):

    - **Sea surface change**: how will tropical ocean warming patterns evolve alongside those at higher latitudes?
    - **Changing weather**: how will hazardous weather change?
    - **Water, carbon and climate**: how will the Earth system respond to human management of the carbon cycle?
    - **Tipping points**: what is the risk of irreversible change under different climate pathways?

??? info "How CMIP7 is organised"

    #### How CMIP7 is organised

    CMIP7 is designed as an evolving framework rather than a single fixed set of experiments:

    - **The DECK**: a small set of baseline experiments that every participating model runs. It is the "entry card" for CMIP.
    - **The [Assessment Fast Track](https://wcrp-cmip.org/cmip-phases/cmip7/fast-track/)**: around 58 priority experiments, including the DECK and future scenarios, timed to feed into IPCC AR7 and national assessments.
    - **Community MIPs**: themed Model Intercomparison Projects (for example, on scenarios, oceans or aerosols) that run on their own timelines. Over 35 are registered.

    All models use shared [forcing datasets](https://wcrp-cmip.org/cmip-phases/cmip7/cmip7-forcing-datasets/) (such as greenhouse gases, aerosols and land use) and produce variables defined in the [CMIP7 Data Request](https://wcrp-cmip.org/cmip7-data-request-v1-2-2-5/).
